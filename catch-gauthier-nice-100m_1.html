The climbing never broke. On steep ground (16–24% grades) you moved at 592 m/h in the first 38 km, 561 m/h through the brutal middle, and 593 m/h after km 80. That is a metronome.

The descents are where the race leaked away. On the same −16% grades your speed fell from 7.6 km/h fresh to 4.7 km/h by mid-race — fitted, each 100 m of descent cost you as much time as 630 m of flat, two and a half times the textbook figure. The 100M has 11,519 m of it.

Race day: when he actually comes through a point, type the clock time into that card’s Arrived box. Every station downstream re-predicts off his real pace, and the uncertainty band restarts from the last confirmed point. Entered times are saved on this device, so the page survives a reload — but each supporter’s phone keeps its own copy; they are not shared between people.

How the prediction works now. The 111K FIT file was cut into 2 km segments and a personal cost model fitted to your moving time: each 100 m of climb costs you like 1.26 km of flat, each 100 m of descent like 0.63 km, and your pace drifted from 0.69× to 0.90× of the fitted mean over the race (R² 0.77). That drift is extended linearly into the extra distance of the 100M — measured in work done, not hours — with a small extra penalty for the second night, which the file cannot know about. Aid-station time is your chosen budget, split across stations in proportion to their size. Earliest and latest widen through the race, roughly −7% to +26% by Plateau St Michel, because that is how uncertainty behaves over two nights.

How live mode works. A confirmed arrival becomes the new anchor: the plan-vs-actual pace ratio up to that point is carried into the remaining legs, weighted by how many hours of evidence it rests on (a 15-minute lead at km 8 moves the forecast a little; the same lead after 14 hours moves it a lot). The uncertainty band collapses to zero at the anchor and regrows toward the finish. Only the latest confirmed point steers — earlier entries stay visible as a record.

What to treat carefully. Distances and cutoffs come from the official profile chart; leg climb/descent from the Trace de Trail GPX, snapped to village coordinates. The fade extension beyond 25 hours is the honest guess in the model: your file proves the first 25 hours, nobody’s file proves hour 40. Cutoff times are barriers, not targets: a red latest-arrival means that pace ends the race.
